# Pricing Approach and Engagement Models

This page covers *how* ACEVA prices and bills work, not what a project costs.
ACEVA does not publish prices, rate cards, hourly or daily rates, or budget
ranges. Every price is quoted per project after the team understands the
requirements.

## Engagement models

ACEVA supports four arrangements, which are already documented and can be
described to a client:

- **Fixed scope** — a defined outcome with written milestones and acceptance criteria.
- **Dedicated team** — continuous work with an assigned team.
- **Hourly** — suits open-ended development, staff augmentation, or ongoing development.
- **Ongoing support** — continuing work after launch.

## How a price is arrived at

ACEVA's documented sequence is to understand the problem first, then prove a
slice of it before committing to a larger budget. The Proof Sprint is a short
paid engagement that solves or demonstrates a specific part of the problem, so
a client can judge the plan and the working relationship before a bigger
commitment.

Nothing is quoted before a senior has reviewed the request.

### Proof Sprint price

ACEVA does not publish a fixed price for a Proof Sprint. It is quoted per
project once the deal is agreed. Payment is split: an advance at the start and
the balance on handover.

### Project estimates and final prices

Estimates and final prices depend on the size of the project and the delivery
timeline. No packages, starting prices or rate cards are published.

### Hourly and daily rates

Hourly or daily rates depend on the contract and the project. The team shares
the details in a proposal after it understands the client's requirements. No
rate, rate card or rate band is published.

### Minimum project size: small jobs and big projects

There is no minimum project size, so ACEVA accepts small jobs as well as big
projects. It builds everything from portfolio websites to full-stack web
applications, ERP and ECM systems, management systems and custom software at
any level.

## Payment schedule and advance payment

The usual payment schedule is 50% at the start and 50% on delivery (handover),
so a client does not pay everything upfront. The terms can be adjusted in the
contract to suit the project.

An advance (deposit) of 30% to 50% is required before work starts. The exact
amount is flexible and agreed with the client. In short, a 50/50 split is the
usual arrangement, and the advance can be lower by agreement.

Invoice due dates (for example net 15 or net 30) are not published; the ACEVA
team can confirm them for each contract at /contact.

## Payment methods and currencies

ACEVA accepts any legal payment method. Accepted currencies are US dollars (USD)
and Pakistani rupees (PKR).

## Hosting, domain and third-party costs

Hosting, domain names and other third-party expenses are not included in the
quoted price. They are billed to the client separately. Whether taxes are
included in or added to a quoted price is not published; the ACEVA team can
confirm it at /contact.

## Work outside the agreed scope

If a request falls outside the agreed requirements — for example a feature the
client wants to add later — ACEVA tells the client that it is not part of the
deal and quotes it separately. The price depends on the
size of the change. Work on it starts only after the client approves the quote.

## Refunds and cancellation

If a client cancels and asks for money back, the refund request is reviewed by
the ACEVA team through a manual process, which can take some time. Conditions depend on the project and are discussed with the
client. ACEVA does not publish a refund guarantee, a fixed refund period or an
automatic refund policy, and Pulse must not imply one.

## What Pulse may say

Pulse may describe the engagement models, the Proof-Sprint-first sequence, the
payment structure, the advance range, the accepted currencies and methods, the
absence of a minimum project size, how out-of-scope work is handled, and how a
refund request is reviewed. For an actual price, rate or budget figure, the
answer is that ACEVA quotes per project after understanding the requirements,
based on the project's size and timeline, and that nothing is quoted before a
senior has reviewed the request. Say that plainly, then point the visitor to
/contact or a Proof Sprint. The absence of a published price is itself the
fact; do not present it as uncertainty on Pulse's part.
