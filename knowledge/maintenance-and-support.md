# Maintenance, Support and Long-Term Upkeep

What ACEVA publishes about post-launch support and maintenance, and general
practice for keeping software modern and secure in the years after launch.

## Post-launch support and maintenance at ACEVA

- ACEVA provides post-launch support. That covers:
  - monitoring;
  - alerts that reach a human;
  - a prioritized improvement list after real usage;
  - named people responsible for bugs.
- Support terms are agreed in writing at launch and can be set in the contract.
- Ongoing support is one of ACEVA's engagement models, alongside fixed scope and a
  dedicated team.

## Warranty on delivered software: bugs fixed after delivery

ACEVA fixes bugs caused by its own development for a set period after delivery,
usually one to two months. The warranty covers defects in ACEVA's own work.
Coverage of new features, third-party services or problems the client causes is
not published, so do not promise it.

## Support response time and SLA

ACEVA's support team typically responds within about 2 hours. This is a typical
response time, not a guaranteed service-level agreement (SLA). Specific support
terms can be agreed in the contract. ACEVA has not published maintenance
packages, a guaranteed SLA or fix times; the team confirms them through
/contact or contact@acevatech.com.

## Hosting and maintenance by ACEVA: fees

If ACEVA hosts and maintains a client's project after launch, a maintenance fee
and a live-server fee apply. The amounts and billing period are not published.

## Keeping software modern and secure 12 to 24 months after launch

ACEVA's Improve phase covers monitoring, iteration, and a modernization roadmap
the client can act on with ACEVA or independently. The code is typed and
documented, so another team can inherit it, and the client keeps ownership of
the accounts. ACEVA has not published a standard upkeep schedule.

## General practice: long-term software upkeep

These are common industry practices, not a published ACEVA plan.

- Apply dependency and security updates regularly, monthly at minimum. Automated
  update pull requests help. Plan major framework and runtime upgrades before
  versions reach end of life. Node.js long-term-support releases, for example,
  have a fixed support window.
- Watch security advisories for every library in use, and patch critical
  vulnerabilities quickly.
- Mobile apps need regular releases even without new features. iOS and Android
  ship major versions every year, and Google Play requires apps to target a
  recent Android API level.
- Test backups by restoring them, not only by taking them.
- Review performance, error rates and costs on a schedule.
- Repeat security reviews periodically.
- Reserve time for paying down technical debt, so the system stays easy to change.
